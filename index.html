How to Use CAS·Analyzer
A step-by-step guide to building, saving, analyzing, and optimizing a compressed air system model.
1 — Build Your System
1
Drag components from the left palette onto the canvas. Start with your compressors, then add treatment equipment (dryer, filter, receiver) in flow order, then loads and leaks.
2
Connect components by dragging from a green output port to a gray input port. Air flows left-to-right by convention. Double-click a connection to delete it.
3
Enter Project Info in the right panel. Project name and customer name are saved with the JSON project file, included in reports, and written to the Excel workbook.
4
Click any component to select it and edit its properties in this panel. Enter CAGI data sheet values for compressors — rated capacity, package input power, rated pressure, and operating pressure.
5
Add Line Loss nodes in series to model distribution pressure drops. Add Regulator nodes to define pressure zones. Add Drain nodes near receivers, dryers, and filters to model condensate removal losses.
Tip: Set Site Conditions (climate zone, elevation) first — they affect compressor capacity correction and moisture analysis throughout.
2 — Run the Analysis
1
Click ▶ Analyze in the header. The engine stages compressors against system demand, propagates flows through the topology, and computes path pressures. see: Staging Logic
2
Review the flow balance in Energy Analysis. Each compressor shows its computed load fraction — this is a result, not an input. If supply exceeds identified demand, the difference is flagged as unaccounted demand (hidden leaks, unmapped loads). see: Flow Balance
3
Check Path Pressure Analysis. Every source→sink path shows delivered pressure vs required pressure with a margin indicator. Red paths mean a load may be starved. see: Path Pressure Drop
4
Toggle Pressure Zone colour (Analysis tab) to visualize where pressure is being spent across the system. Blue = high pressure, orange = low pressure.
5
Review Pressure Reduction Opportunity. The tool finds the minimum header pressure that satisfies all loads and quantifies the energy savings available per psi of reduction. see: Pressure Reduction Analysis
6
Review Moisture & Drains. Condensate load at the aftercooler, dryer effectiveness, condensation risk downstream, and annual drain air loss cost. see: Moisture Model
3 — Compressor Inputs Explained
CAGI Rated Pressure vs Operating Pressure
These are two distinct values. CAGI Rated Pressure is the discharge pressure printed on the data sheet — the condition under which rated flow and power were measured. Operating Pressure is the actual controller setpoint your system runs at. Most systems run 10–20 psi below the CAGI rated pressure. The tool corrects power for the difference using isentropic scaling. see: Isentropic Pressure Correction
Staging Priority
Priority 1 = base load unit (fills demand first, runs at highest load fraction). Priority 2 = first trim unit, and so on. The staging engine fills demand from the lowest priority number upward. A warning appears if any unit's computed load fraction falls below its minimum stable load threshold. see: Staging Logic
Control Type
Load/Unload (L/U): cycles between full load and unload power. Efficient at high loads, wasteful at part load. Modulating: throttles inlet valve — power stays high even at low flow. VSD: varies motor speed to match demand — power drops near-cubically with flow reduction. see: Part-Load Power Curves
Load fraction is always computed from system demand — you cannot enter it directly. To force a specific operating point, adjust loads until the staging engine produces the desired result.
4 — Pressure Zones & Regulators
A Regulator node drops pressure to a fixed setpoint for a downstream zone. It passes full downstream demand flow with no restriction, provided upstream pressure exceeds the setpoint plus upstream margin (default 10 psi). If upstream pressure is insufficient, a "droop" warning fires.
Use regulators to protect low-pressure loads while running the header at a higher pressure for loads that need it — or to unlock header pressure reduction by isolating the one load that needs higher pressure.
Tip: After adding regulators, re-run Analyze and check the Pressure Reduction Opportunity panel. Regulators at high-pressure loads often allow significant header pressure reduction.
5 — 8760 Annual Simulation
1
Open Detailed Mode from the header. The left side contains schedules and component tools, the center is the case canvas, and the right side shows case summary or component characteristics.
2
Define schedules for compressor availability and load activity. Compressor schedules are on/off per block; load schedules scale demand by the block factor, so a 62% block runs the load at 62% of its duty-adjusted demand. Schedule calculations use the hour of day and ignore minutes.
3
Assign equipment to schedules in the Equipment Assignment panel. All compressors default to Compressor Schedule 1; all loads default to Load Schedule 1.
4
Duplicate Pre→Post to create the post-improvement case. The Post canvas can show the Pre case as a faded reference layer. Move, edit, delete, reconnect, or replace compatible components to define the proposed system.
5
Use Change Tools on a selected Post component to apply a compatible replacement. Compatible connections and schedule assignments are preserved where possible, while editable equipment parameters are cleared so the replacement must be defined.
6
Run Simulation to compute all 8,760 hours. Results show annual energy (MWh), peak demand (kW), supply volume (Mscf), leak loss, average pressure, and specific energy (kWh/kscf). Pre and Post are compared side-by-side. see: 8760 Simulation Method
7
Export results with Pre/Post CSV downloads or Export to Excel. The Excel workbook includes 8,760 formula rows for Pre and Post, annual savings formulas, demand reduction formulas, assumptions, warnings, QC checks that flag differences over 1%, and a System Diagram sheet with an embedded canvas-style image.
6 — Save, Load & Project Files
Use Save Project in the header to download a JSON project file that can be loaded later with Load Project. The file stores project/customer info, components, connections, drain attachments, site conditions, schedules, equipment assignments, post case data, and change groups.
Saved project filenames include the CAS project file indicator and version, such as plant_air_study_CAS-v1_2026-08-18.json. The JSON also contains schema and version fields so future JSON types can be identified safely.
7 — Reports
Generate PDF-style reports from the Scenarios tab. Management Summary is a one-page executive report with headline numbers and top recommendations. Engineering Report includes full component inventory, flow balance table, path pressure analysis, and energy summary. Use the browser Print function (Ctrl+P / Cmd+P) to save as PDF.
For calculation review, use Export to Excel in Detailed Mode. The workbook is designed for modern Excel and reproduces the 8760 energy and demand calculations with visible formulas, web-vs-Excel QC checks, project metadata, and a generated System Diagram image.
Methods & Calculations
Technical reference for all calculations performed by CAS·Analyzer. Click any section to expand. Links in the Guide tab open the relevant section directly.
M1 — Site Correction Factor
Adjusts compressor rated flow capacity for site conditions (elevation, temperature) relative to CAGI standard test conditions.
Equation
corr = (P_site / P_ref) × √(T_ref / T_site)
P_site = site ambient pressure (psia)
P_ref = CAGI rated inlet pressure (psia, from data sheet)
T_ref = CAGI rated inlet temperature (°R = °F + 459.67)
T_site = site ambient temperature (°R)
Application
Applied to rated flow only: actual_flow = rated_flow × corr × load_fraction. Not applied to power — power is determined by isentropic work at the operating pressure ratio and load fraction, independent of inlet density.
Assumptions
Inlet density scales linearly with pressure and inversely with the square root of absolute temperature (ideal gas). Compressor volumetric efficiency is constant across the site condition range.
Reference: ISO 1217:2009 Displacement Compressors — Acceptance Tests, Annex C (Site condition correction).
M2 — Isentropic Pressure Correction
Corrects compressor package input power for operation at a discharge pressure different from the CAGI rated test pressure.
Equation
scale = [(P_op / P_in)^K − 1] / [(P_rated / P_in)^K − 1]

actual_power = rated_power × scale × part_load_factor

K = (k−1)/k = 0.286   (k = 1.4 for dry air)
P_op = operating discharge pressure (psia = gauge + 14.696)
P_rated = CAGI rated discharge pressure (psia)
P_in = CAGI rated inlet pressure (psia, typically 14.696)
k = ratio of specific heats for dry air (1.4)
Pressure Reduction Savings
savings_per_psi = rated_power × PLF × K × (P_op/P_in)^(K−1) / [P_in × iso_rated] × hours × rate
This is the analytical derivative d(power)/d(P_op) evaluated at the current operating point — more accurate than the rule-of-thumb 0.5%/psi approximation, particularly at lower pressures.
Assumptions
Isentropic compression with dry air (k constant at 1.4). Package mechanical and motor efficiency constant across the operating pressure range. Valid for operating pressures within ±30% of rated.
Reference: ISO 1217:2009; Compressed Air Challenge — Fundamentals of Compressed Air Systems (2016), Section 3.
M3 — Part-Load Power Curves
Models the relationship between compressor load fraction and power consumption for each control strategy.
Load/Unload (L/U)
PLF = f × 1.0 + (1−f) × p_ul

f = load fraction (0–1)    p_ul = unload power fraction (default 0.20)
The compressor cycles between full-load power and unload power. At 50% average load with 20% unload power, PLF = 0.5×1.0 + 0.5×0.20 = 0.60. Unload power represents the power consumed running unloaded (compressing to atmosphere, no useful output).
Modulating (Inlet Throttle)
PLF = 0.30 + 0.70 × f
Inlet modulation throttles the inlet valve to reduce flow. Power does not drop proportionally — the machine still turns at full speed. At 50% load, PLF = 0.65 (65% of rated power for 50% of rated flow).
Variable Speed Drive (VSD)
PLF = f ^ 1.3
VSD reduces motor speed to match demand. Power drops near-cubically. At 50% load, PLF = 0.5^1.3 ≈ 0.41 (41% of rated power). Exponent 1.3 reflects real-world rotary screw performance (theoretical cubic applies to ideal fans, not compressors with fixed pressure ratio).
Minimum Stable Load Defaults
L/U: 30% • Modulating: 40% • VSD: 20%. Below these thresholds a staging warning is issued. User-adjustable per machine.
Reference: Compressed Air Challenge — Advanced Management of Compressed Air Systems (2016); CAGI Data Sheet methodology.
M4 — Compressor Staging Logic
Determines each compressor's operating load fraction from system demand using priority-based staging. Load fraction is a computed result, not a user input.
Algorithm
1. total_demand = load_demand + leak_flow + drain_loss
2. Sort online compressors by staging priority (1 = base load)
3. For each unit in order:
if remaining ≥ rated_cap: unit runs at 100%, remaining -= rated_cap
else: unit is trim unit, load_frac = remaining / rated_cap, remaining = 0
4. Units beyond demand: L/U stays at unload power; VSD/MOD go to zero
Unaccounted Demand
When total staged supply exceeds identified demand + leaks + drains, the difference is reported as unaccounted demand. This indicates unmapped loads, unreported leaks, purge/blow-off points, or other consumption not modelled in the diagram.
Minimum Stable Load Warning
If a trim unit's computed load fraction falls below its minimum stable load threshold, a warning is issued suggesting the unit be staged off. This prevents modelling unrealistic operating points.
Reference: Compressed Air Challenge — Best Practices for Compressed Air Systems, Chapter 7 (Compressor Controls and Control Strategies).
M5 — Flow Balance & Topology Engine
Propagates actual flow and capacity through the system diagram using a directed acyclic graph (DAG) traversal.
Topology Validation
A depth-first search (DFS) with node colouring (white/gray/black) detects cycles before analysis. Ring mains are not supported — the tool requires a tree or DAG topology.
Attributed Demand (Reverse Pass)
At each merge node: attr[conn] = total_demand_at_node × cap[conn] / total_cap_into_node
A reverse topological pass attributes downstream demand back to each upstream connector proportional to its capacity fraction. This ensures each compressor is assigned the portion of demand it is responsible for based on its relative rated capacity at merge points.
Forward Flow Pass
flow[conn] = nodeActualOut[from] × attr[conn] / total_attr_leaving_from
Actual flow on each connector is the upstream node's actual output multiplied by the attributed demand fraction. This correctly handles parallel sources (compressors merging), parallel loads (loads splitting), and mixed topologies.
Reference: Standard graph theory — topological sort (Kahn's algorithm); Hardy-Cross network analysis principles applied to DAG topology.
M6 — Path Pressure Drop
Computes the pressure delivered at each load by summing losses along each source→sink path through treatment equipment, line losses, and regulators.
Component Pressure Drop (Treatment Equipment)
dP_actual = dP_rated × (Q_actual / Q_rated)²
dP_rated = rated pressure drop from component data (psi)
Q_rated = rated flow capacity of component (scfm)
Q_actual = actual flow through component at operating point (scfm)
Pressure drop through filters, dryers, and similar devices scales with the square of flow velocity (Darcy-Weisbach regime). At part-load, actual dP is significantly lower than rated dP.
Line Loss (Flow-Scaled)
dP_actual = dP_rated × (Q_actual / Q_rated)²
Same quadratic scaling as treatment equipment. Represents pipe friction losses, fittings, and distribution system losses. Measured mode applies a fixed dP regardless of flow.
Regulator
dP = P_upstream − P_setpoint   (when P_upstream > P_setpoint + P_margin)
A pressure regulator maintains downstream pressure at its setpoint regardless of upstream pressure variation, provided upstream exceeds setpoint plus minimum upstream margin. No flow restriction in normal operation. Below the margin threshold, a droop warning is issued.
Pressure Margin
margin = P_delivered − P_required ≥ 0 (required for adequate supply)
Reference: ISO 6358 (pneumatic flow through components); Compressed Air Challenge — Best Practices, Chapter 5 (Distribution Systems).
M7 — Pressure Reduction Opportunity
Identifies the minimum header pressure that satisfies all loads and quantifies energy savings from reducing operating pressure.
Minimum Required Header Pressure
P_min = max over all load paths of (P_required_load + sum(dP along path))
The binding constraint is the load with the highest sum of required pressure plus path losses. All other loads have margin. Reducing header pressure below P_min starves the binding load.
Savings per psi (Isentropic Derivative)
d(Power)/d(P_op) = rated_power × PLF × K × (P_op/P_in)^(K−1) / [P_in × iso_rated_term]
This is the analytical derivative of the isentropic scaling factor with respect to operating pressure, evaluated at the current operating point. More accurate than the commonly cited 0.5%/psi rule of thumb, particularly at pressures significantly above or below the CAGI rated pressure.
Reference: Compressed Air Challenge — Fundamentals, Module 5; ISO 1217:2009.
M8 — Moisture & Condensate Model
Simplified psychrometric model for condensate load estimation and condensation risk assessment.
Saturation Pressure (Antoine Equation)
ln(P_sat_Pa) = 16.3872 − 3885.70 / (T_K − 43.64)
P_sat_psia = P_sat_Pa × 0.000145038
T_K = temperature in Kelvin = (°F − 32) × 5/9 + 273.15
Humidity Ratio
W = 0.622 × P_w / (P_total − P_w)
P_w = (RH/100) × P_sat
W = humidity ratio (lb water / lb dry air)
P_w = partial pressure of water vapour (psia)
P_total = total air pressure (psia)
Aftercooler Condensate Rate
condensate = (W_inlet − W_aftercooler_sat) × 0.075 × 60 × 0.12  (gal/hr per scfm)
Air density at standard conditions ≈ 0.075 lb/scf. 1 lb water = 0.12 gal. Aftercooler outlet assumed saturated at aftercooler outlet temperature. Moisture removed = difference in humidity ratios.
Condensation Risk (Downstream of Dryer)
Risk is assessed by comparing ambient pipe temperature (assumed equal to ambient air temperature) to the pressure dewpoint of the air leaving the dryer. LOW: ambient > PDP + 15°F. MEDIUM: ambient > PDP. HIGH: ambient ≤ PDP (condensation likely in distribution piping).
Reference: ASHRAE Fundamentals Handbook (2021), Chapter 1 (Psychrometrics); Compressed Air Challenge — Best Practices, Chapter 6 (Air Treatment).
M9 — Drain Air Loss Model
Quantifies compressed air wasted through condensate drains using subcritical orifice flow.
Orifice Flow Equation
Q = 0.65 × π/4 × d² × P_gauge × 0.0685  (scfm)
d = orifice diameter (inches)
P_gauge = line gauge pressure (psig)
0.65 = discharge coefficient (C_d) for sharp-edged orifice
0.0685 = empirical constant for air at standard conditions (subcritical flow)
Timer Drain
Q_avg = Q_orifice × (t_on / t_cycle)
Air is wasted during the open interval regardless of condensate level. t_on = on-time per cycle (seconds). t_cycle = total cycle time (seconds).
Float/Mechanical Drain
Normal condition: zero air waste (opens only when condensate is present). Failed-open condition: equivalent to timer drain at 100% duty cycle — continuous air loss at full orifice flow.
Manual Drain
Q_avg = Q_orifice × (opens_per_day × duration_min) / 1440
Reference: Compressed Air Challenge — Best Practices, Chapter 6; Parker Hannifin — Compressed Air Treatment Engineering Handbook.
M10 — 8760 Annual Simulation
Simulates compressed air system performance for all 8,760 hours of a calendar year by applying schedules, staging compressors against hourly demand, and aggregating results.
Calendar Construction
Each hour is classified by day of week (Mon–Sun) and whether it falls on a US federal holiday. Federal holidays are computed algorithmically using the standard rules (e.g. MLK Day = 3rd Monday of January, Thanksgiving = 4th Thursday of November, observed dates shift for weekend holidays).
Schedule Application
compressor: on/off based on schedule block covering this hour
load demand: Q_load × duty_cycle% × schedule_factor%
Compressor schedules are strictly on/off — load fraction is determined by the staging engine from demand, not by the schedule. Load schedules scale demand by the schedule block factor, so a 62% block runs the load at 62% of its duty-adjusted demand.
Hourly Calculation
For each hour:
1. Sum load demand + leak flow (if any compressor on) + drain losses
2. Stage online compressors against total demand (M4)
3. Compute system kW = compressor power + dryer power
4. Heat rejection = compressor shaft power × 0.85
5. Delivered pressure = operating pressure − path losses at actual flow
Annual Aggregations
Energy (MWh) = Σ system_kW ÷ 1000
Supply volume (Mscf) = Σ supply_scfm × 60 ÷ 1000
Spec. energy (kWh/kscf) = total_kWh / (total_scf / 1000)
Heat rejection coefficient of 0.85 is a typical value for air-cooled rotary screw packages (85% of shaft power rejected as heat; ~15% in compressed air). Specific energy (kWh/kscf) is the primary annual efficiency metric — lower is better.
Reference: Compressed Air Challenge — Advanced Management; ENERGY STAR Compressed Air System Assessment Protocol.
M11 — Energy & Cost Calculations
Annual Energy Cost
cost = total_kW × operating_hours × electricity_rate
Specific Package Power
SPP = actual_power (kW) / (actual_flow / 100)   [kW per 100 scfm]
SPP is the primary CAGI efficiency metric. Lower is better. Typical rotary screw values at 100 psi: 14–18 kW/100 scfm at full load. SPP increases at part load for L/U and modulating types; decreases for VSD below ~70% load.
System Productive Efficiency
efficiency = productive_demand_scfm / total_supply_scfm × 100%
Productive demand excludes leaks and drain losses. A well-optimized system targets >85% productive efficiency. Values below 75% indicate significant waste streams.
Reference: CAGI Data Sheet standard; Compressed Air Challenge — Fundamentals, Module 2.
